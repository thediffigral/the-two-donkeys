Written on 2026-08-31, after going through The Owl the River and the Valley - Arupa Patangia Kalita.

the own-ness

 I have been reading a new book lately since the day one of my friends, rahul, rent me one. he came to my room, took away one from my collection, and then gave me this 'the owl, the river, and the valley' by Arupa Patangia Kalita, translated by Mitra Phukan. he has liked the stories in here a lot, and specially he requested me to read two of his favorite ones. he has not yet finished reading it and according to him, he has not finished it on his own, because these stories so refreshes his soul that he wants to preserve it whenever he will feel 'hungry'. 

however, till i can recall, i have read assamese books in Assamese only, not as translated works. i may have read some articles probably, but books or stories - i don't remember at present. this is the first book perhaps, which i am reading in another language despite being i can access the stories in Assamese itself. i could not think about the feeling it can give me. i started the book, with the translator's note where she is introducing from the root of the language, the Assamese society and how Arupa Kalita belongs to a generation of writers whose work shaped the Assamese literature. like any other translator hubmly acknowledging the limitation one can feel because the local 'feel' is anyway is hardly possible to carry. nonetheless, i also thought i would complete the book (it features 12 stories as far as i remember) then talk about it, but then decided ki no, i will write about it with the 5 stories i have read so far and because i like all of them. the stories indeed has the 'assamese' vibe, atleast i could sense it. and mitra phukan has been kind enough to include the assamese ness in the dialogues, like 'you need to carry this apple, dei' - the last word is very assamese inclusion and i find it interesting when i found that amidst the english descriptions. the stories are not that long however, the length didnot come into mind when i was reading. only one story (the 2nd one) is long enough that it needs subsections. the yellow flip-flops and the rajmao - these two stories shaken me quite a bit that i had to stay still and quiet for a bit and had to grasp the scenario. probably i need to explore arupa kalita's stories more. 


-- Male Donkey.
