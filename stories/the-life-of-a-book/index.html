the life of a book

Damn! just finished reading gene machine.. when i am saying just, i meant literally just, like 60 seconds ago. i just am not able to hold the rush of hormones after completing the story. this was not in my plan. i thought of reading chapter by chapter. the lower back pain came as blessing in disguise and i could complete the last few chapters in one shot - and i now feel it had to be done this way - in one solo ride.

the last chapter i read where the nobel prize and the discussions related to it was there. i remember writing about a thing that i was not able to recall back then, it was the academic ego venki wrote. nonetheless, in the following chapters, he was working on the problem again and attending conferences. in the usual writing style, he proceeded the story. the conference stories where the rival gang takes more and more time to tell about their findings, more on the speculation sometimes and the insider politics, and then the interesting thing about their findings getting published in nature and science - when i was reading it, it feels so normal that these people only talks abut nature and science, it is so common for them. in one paragraph, venki talks about reviewing a journal article for science, where when he discovers that some task which he wanted to do from his lab, was done by somebody else. this is one aspect even i thought when somebody submits a journal article, what if the reviewer takes the idea and do the work and reject the submitted article taking a long time. i guess this is usually not done, however, i am not sure if i am 100% correct in it.

the description on the possible nobel prize things and related events are what was a very new experience for me. when i was reading it, it felt like somebody very known or a close friend was on the journey. while i knew from the book itself that venki was a nobel awardee, it is always fescinating to know the journey. the new students joining his lab, one particular i remember was a married lady: who later got pregnant and venki thought now many tasks would be postponed because she would take maternity leave. she did, but she was passionate enough to complete her tasks. i was really inspired learning other students stories too, where one came with trending fashionable attire, but did astonishingly well in the interview that venki took him. one another student, who right after joining asked him for somedays off, because he wanted to roam around/travel. venki thought he had made a huge blunder, however, later got corrected when the student returned and showed his skill and dedication into research. 

the chapter where he got the phone call from nobel committee is worth-reading. the description of the events of the day, the phone call, and the aftermath - never in my life i thought i would get a first hand experience of such a huge honor. he was informed 30 minutes before the news was public and was allowed only to share the news with his wife. however, his wife was out for walks. while he was on the phone, his two colleagues overheard and opened the champagne  they reserved for the next science publication. when his wife got to know about it, she simply said she was sure that he is smart enough to secure one of those prizes. he quoted from somebody queen (i forgot the name) 'behind every successful man, there is a surprised woman'.

the day of receiving the prize, the talk he presented - everything is described. he shared since long time ago he had been reading the nobel prize notes and now he is preparing the one to be presented. when he was receiving the prize, how bored the king looked because he has been doing it every year. there is also another account of how India and Indian people flooded with emails after he received the prize and he got annoyed by some of them because he has long lost his citizenship and also acc to him nationality is by accident (the statement for which he got backlashed). how some 'pundits' were discussing over whether he could achieve this by settling in india, also some journalists asked him if he was offered directorship of indian institutes- for which he was even annoyed more. later he was awarded the highest civilian honor as he wrote and also knighthood and president of royal society. 

i was still wondering on him sharing his perspectives on his rivals, like Ada, with whom he shared the nobel on structures and functions of ribosome. i am not sure how it was decided to include his straight opinions on her works. i am not sure if i could write and publish directly on things such as 'my friend did this, but i doubt this was his original idea'. i don't know. 

this was a book when i was reading i forgot to look at the page number. the chapters was not so long, not short- they were crisp. the acknowledgement chapter was after the book is ended and he also added and notes and references. one funny thing is he added many pictures, scientific diagrams, photos of them doing experiments, conferences, scientists, his rivals, nobel prize stage - many and all of them was labelled as Figures, just like we do in scientific articles. 

i feel good with my decision of reading the book. i wish i had read it before i join PhD, many of my life aspects would change and perhaps i could contribute more to science as a whole. nonetheless, it's never to late to do anything. all my gratitude to Venki Ramakrishnan. 


-- Male Donkey.
