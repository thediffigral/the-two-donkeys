Written on 2026-08-01, after going through The Village By The Sea - Anita Desai.

looking out to the sea

 it was really not planned, but I have finished reading a novel, the village by the sea by Anita Desai. I did not know such book exist untill i picked the copy and bought it instantly from a local book store which I hardly do now-a-days, mostly because the price is lowered if I buy it from online market, and also because the soft copy can be accessed in the internet-the practice, although, is not morally correct if you are having it for free. this is the first time i have met Anita Desai, curious enough, i read about her in the book itself. the book has it written award winning novel, however, which award they did not mention. i like the cover art and in the back cover the publisher has written 2-3 lines on what the book is going to be about. i understood Desai is actually a big shot and from the acknowledgement part, i readily understood she wrote one book just to pay honor to her days in a village by a sea near bombay. how writeous you have to be that you complete writing a book to let the world know what you felt about the time you really enjoy being.. I don't know but i admire it a lot. 

the book is about a family living in the coast and their stories revolving in the village. it's a sweet story, like similar template kind we actually come across on a day-to-day basis, and comes into our notice only when somebody presented it before us in an unique way. Desai is successful in that entirely (however I still believe that she didnot write it to impress the reader,  and i can not judge her on that basis). the book is divided into 12 or 13 chapters, with each chpater has its own subsections separated inside. Probably i also picked up the book because i am having a curiosity grown inside to know the local stories of indian villages more and through the lens of indian english writers. yes, definitely i will pick a book if it has a story based on a remote village next, to know them, to know their unique enviroment yet similar humanly feeling. 

the speed of the book is steady, average and it gets you going. its around 250 page long novel. i could guess the positive ending of the story (which definitely had to be) and so sometimes I thought i would go slow on the book (means I would end it later, read one chapter a day). I terminated this plan. 

having praised the language of the book, i also noticed one thing- while i was reading everything about the nearby village of the sea and everything, i did not get enough motivation to see the village, probably because Desai focused more on the description of the events. it does feature some description for sure, however, many times i felt it like a movie with not so accurate color grading. Although there is 5-6 percentage chance that I may miss the beauty because i read it fast. however, this point can be defied too as the descritpion of coconut tree, sea side and all I could vividly picturised, however there still lacks some extra elements that was required for me to have gained enough excitement to go see the actual place. 

I just thought and maybe figured it out, maybe-- maybe, the thing about the extra motivation may come from my inner expectations too. While I 'watched' the movie kambalangi nights, i instantly wanted to go that village. probably while I was reading this book, i craved a similar picturisation in my mind. because i already had this so itched in my mind, i may have missed some amount of beauty of the Thul village from this novel or i completely fail to load this in my imagination thouroughly or replace it. this  however i think is normal because that was a movie, and this is a book. 

i will definitely recommend the book. some english i don't know, was used in a manner i am not familiar with or i previously thought was grammatically incorrect. for example, when somebody possess something and we ask if they posses it, we ask, 'do you have this thing?' in the book, Desai writes, 'have you this thing?' - i could not be sure about it why it is written that way. i want to correct me in this if I am not learnt this way (too lazy to google search it). I am sure that was not used colloquially.

I want to thank Desai for the beautiful creation and let the reader know how beautiful small stories can be!. 


-- Male Donkey
