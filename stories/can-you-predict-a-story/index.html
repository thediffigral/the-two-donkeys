Written on 2026-07-05, after going through Days at the Morisaki Bookshop - Satoshi Yagisawa.

can you predict a story !

When you read a book (or even watch a movie), the curiosity in you always devote some of your imagination to predict what is going to happen later, how the story will take turn. There is a joy in being correct. You feel like a winner. But, sometimes the writer proves you to be completely wrong. You predict something the story take a complete different turn. In that case, sometimes you are disappointed with the story, and in other times you can do nothing but falling in love with the story even more. For me (and I guess for everyone), the later makes your reading even more interesting. And it is the power of the creativity the writers use (anyway every person will have a different way of imagination). I am experiencing exactly that with this book, "Days at the Morisaki BookShop". The story is not very unpredictable, like completely not aligning with what I have imagined it to be. But the unexpected turns that the life of uncle Satoru and Takako ( I finally remember her name) is taking is literally keeping me hooked to the book. I am, in my imagination roaming around the streets of Jimbocho, I am taking a sip of coffee at 'Saveur' enjoying my evening with that soft piano music and the fresh aroma of coffee with a secondhand ( and maybe new) book in my hand. One thing I always see in the movies and also read in books that people go to coffee shops with books, they sat alone, read their book and enjoy coffee. I very much romaticize that thing. I doubt if there will be any place in  here where I can do the same without the fear of being judged.  Some people read everywhere, in cafes, in trains,  in flight, even in buses. I find those people very magnanimous. Instead of scrolling through our phones, cannot we make this our new normal !

One more thing I want to write for today. As I previously mentioned, my liking for uncle Satoru's character is growing with every chapter I read. Lately, I found out that the bond between him and his wife is less like a married couple and more like old friends. That's exactly resonate with what I personally want from my partner. And so, my love for uncle Satoru and Momoko has found yet another reason to grow.
