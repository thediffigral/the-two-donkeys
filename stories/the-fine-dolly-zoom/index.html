Written on 2026-09-14, after going through Kokadeutar Har - Nabakanta Baruah.

the fine dolly zoom

I could not stick to my promise, and finished Kokadeutar har. I thought i would go on it slow, grasp each words-sentences slow, but the pace of the novel is dynamic and you can not plan it to read it on multiple sittings. after the 3rd chapter I think, the novel turns into a well executed movie and you just can't deny the fact that the novel indeed reflects the people's history from Assam, from the period before British. the story is engaging and there is no unnecessary description. While I disagree with the view that it is a finest thriller novel written in Assamese, i personally feel it more like socio-political one, with maximum flavor of Assamese-ness intact. There is one favorite part in the writing style I particularly would like to mention. the core story of it is a reminiscent of an old woman, who is describing the horrific story as the reason why his grand children should not marry from a particular family. so, the initial 1-2 chapters are there organized in such a way that there is the present, then past, then again the readers are arched back to present, some questions are posed, and then the subsequent chapters belonged to past. then after almost 7-8 chapters i think, to the ending of one chapter: while the chapter is based on the past, there is one sentence by the old woman from the present and we are taken back to the present again. I was astound to see my mind naturally adapt to the transition with the description. had it been a movie, it definitely would be a dissolve-like transition, where we see the pictures of the past and then slowly an old woman from the present appearing slowly and finally becomes visible. the sentence was sudden, yet we are not harmed by the flow. 

I have not read Nabakanta Baruah before as far as I remember. This is one fine choice  i have made. the novel is so concise and to-the-point that its sole purpose is directly to convey the story and let people enjoy it without any delay. even now when I think of it, i feel it like during our childhood, the mobile theatres had their dramas welcomed by another short dance-drama. then once the dance-drama ended, the main drama scene starts. many-a-times, to grab the people's attention directly from the dance-drama to the main drama, the introductory scene had suspenseful music, the scene many-a-times involves a murder or something that poses question in audience's mind 'what happened what happened'. the book starts directly with similar suspense and carry it forward in the similar velocity to the end. A worth reading. In my limited readership experience, I thought Nabakanta Baruah is a fine poet, but then discovering this was a huge blessing for me. I will try to read some more from him. Thank you for this. 

-- Male Donkey.
