Written on 2026-11-03, after going through A Little History of Philosophy.

syllabus

This book is keeping a hook at the end of each chapter: like stories being knitted with the same thread. The first one was on Socrates and Plato, and the following is on Aristotle. After reading the chapter, first, i learnt a new word, eudaimonia (spells you-die-monia), a word used for the true happiness. According to Aristotle, true happiness is found only after you spend a considerable amount of time in it. I felt like every other happy moments are only short-span, a true happiness is but difficult. The theory also includes the possible out-of-control unhappiness moments, for example, your mood is harmed when the people you care about is suffering from something that causes trouble. To chase true happiness or eudaimonia, only one has to live the entire life  bring a good person - i could only get that. However what is the right thing to do or good thing to do, the chapter has no direct answer. I think, in the end philosophy ultimately gets stuck in this question only, what is the right to do. Many of things in this book are ethics lessons.
