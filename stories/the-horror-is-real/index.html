the horror is real

the next chapter is equally more frightening yet beautiful, 'honour'. a glimpse can easily be realised following the previous chapter and what exactly is going to be found by looking at the name of the chapter only. the chapter is almost 70 pages long. it features interviews of different persons, both male-female perspectives. it can be taken as the continuation of the previous one, where people were considering the honor of the community mostly carried by the women, and the ironly is when it comes to rescuing them, they were thinking twice before embracing them. it has some mentions of a RSS led magazines where some RSS author trying to portray the parition as a harm to Bharat Mata, a piece of land is given to Pakistan is actually like tearing apart a body part from Bharat Mata, let alone the worst conditions of the women abducted. While in reality, as much as the hindu women had to go though such devastating condition, similar events were also faced by the muslim women. Howver, the narratives definitely did not include what indian man were doing to pak woman. while i was reading, i got equally interested (curious actually) what can be the perspective of pakistan people on the parition issue. For sure i am not going to find it in Indian market.  

the chapter features several haunting stories about the conditions woman had to go through and how a sikh father had to slit their daugghters head. i mean i could not imagine the scene. and there features another story where the son is describing  women who had died before any muslim person took them away, as martyrs. the irony is his own mother, although she tried to took her life jumping into a well right after her two daughter died in the similar manner, could not die and she was still alive. and the son, when he was describing (interviewing), he did not told them (the author) her to be his mother. he just spit out the name only. Urvashi Butalia only came to know about the connection b/w them when she interviewed the mother and only then she came to know about their relation.  i mean i could not think of it how and why religion was (is) so into people's head. the son did not talk much about his mother just because she survived and she is not the martyr he should be proud of. 

i thought of one  question regarding this,  which however rn i forgot. Even while I was reading, i almost forgetting it only then i wrote it with a pencil. i will edit the post once i can recall or atleast see the note. Probably it was related to the religion and how/why it administered peoples head in such a deeper way that they only think of preserving the honor when it comes to religion and not other (such as my sister is illiterate that is okay, but she is married to some other religion - that wont be okay). I have to look at the exact one.

the description (actually it is the direct transcription of those interviewd) is really haunting. once the chapter is finished i felt relived a bit. Since i completed reading a really long chapter, out of curiosity, i checked what the next chapter could be and i got frightened even more. the chapters name is children.
