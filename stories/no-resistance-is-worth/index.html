no resistance is worth

I re-read till page 10 about threee-times, yet at first i could not engage myself with the first chapter, 1599, of willliam's book. maybe i was not quiet ready to start the book or i was taking the book at hand out of habit. in the first reading, i could go upto first section of the chapter. similar to the book by urvashi, this chapter also has sections ended by a logo (this was also the case in the book by anita desai). in the next reading, i went upto page 10 i guess, then the third reading, i was determined yet, could go upto 15. then i thought, come on, lets just break the barrier. i started from the very first line again. today while i was rethinking about it, i think it is the language of the book probably i was not familiar to. but that maynot also be correct, because as i proceeded, the things began making sense to me. while the length of the chapter is 55 pages, however, the fontsize is really small. so, the book is not pleasure worthy if you read a scanned one. probably an e-book can justify. 

the chapter starts off with a comparison, shakespear is thinking about writing one of his immoral creation and on the other, the moment before the birth of east india company. i could never thought of knowing the formation of this company. many times i often mistake myself regarding the british-raj, i keep on forgetting that it was just a company. the post-math of forming the company is really interesting and consideration of the aspects of rival countries/communities, what to do business, and how the sailing will take place - it's all there. and amidst all the resistance and backfires, how they finally landed to India, because India has all three of their requirement for business, cotton, indigo and one other i just forgot now. the author includes the original writings in between to show the authenticity of his description. i could see the spellings are also a bit different.(mogol = mughal is just an indian example, there were other english words too, it is due to the british accent i think). there were various other things that cut through my memory, like how bajirao portrayed himself as the god and was determined to clear the 'demon' muslims, the son of jahangir who tried to remove the hindus and hindu land to muslim - probably it is because of him actually the war b/w hindu muslim took place, how the english when they tried to do business in bombay and their natives call them benchoot and bettichoot (yes these words are there  - i was so laughing out loud) .. and also how the english when they were trying to get permission to do business were treated in mogol courts - it was so interesting. in an account where one english resisted pamagamy(i think this is the right word - satidah protha) and married the young lady who were about to be burnt with her deceased husband. and all about the maratha- mughal war, and then how one unstable english without any strategy fought against mughal only to find out how strong the mughals were. but then in the race to win over the local administration and similarly make their business ease and perform better than rivalry group (for example, the portugese was there, who happened to send the kings special gifts), the overall situation was out of hand. Even the company had to manage everything in britain as well because there is an account where the EIC is so grown that they can now even bribe the members of pariliament to make laws in favor of the company. looking forward for the next chapter.
