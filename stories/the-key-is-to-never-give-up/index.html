Written on 2026-09-20, after going through The Forest of Enchantments-Chitra Banerjee.

The key is to never give up !

 Finally, again after a long time. I don't want to admit, but it's becoming a personality trait I have. I start something with excitement and then even though I like it, I tend to forget in when I get busy with something else, and I never pick it up again. That's been a constant thing for me throughout my life. That's the reason why I can never really do something, until and unless I have some obligations. When I skip it for some days some strange kind to feeling arise from inside, that constantly remind me that oh, I have already skip it for this long, I already quit that habit, and I most of the time never start again, until I get a push. I know it's nothing like a good trait. With my reading habit too, it's kind of the same thing. I got busy in work and then I forgot to read and write. But, this time I thought, I will break that chain of procrastination. I told myself, its okay that I am not regular, but I am not going to quit. And with this, last night I finished the book I was reading, "The forest of Enchantments" by Chitra Banerjee ( I have not read the rest of the book in one day itself, I was reading some pages in my initial days of not writing the blog, then I quit both reading and writing for may be last two weeks). That gave me some kind of joy, I have finished three book this year. I know it's too less, but yeah I am happy because it's more than reading zero books a year. For me at this point, building the habit of reading is way more important than the number its elf. 


So, about the book, I really enjoyed reading. In Sita, too some extent, I found a role model. The way the Sita's character judge the incidents happening around her with both logic and compassion is what I want to gain for myself. When I first started reading the book, Ram was such a nice person, a loving husband, a perfect son and a very righteous man. I mentioned in one of my previous blogs that my entire life I always looked at Sri Krishna as a God, never Ram. I was thinking that I may start considering Ram too, after reading this book. The way Sita loved Ram, and the small incidents of love and romance between Sita, the way he cared for Sita during their forest life did grow a corner of affection in my heart. But, after the war with Ravan ends, things changed for me. As an woman, I could bear the injustice Ram has done to Sita, for being the righteous ruler for his people. Undoubtedly, he loved Sita, by doing injustice to Sita, emotionally, he suffered more than anyone. But, was it necessary! The example that Ram has set is still running in our society in some sense or other. We still believe, being a good person means you have to sacrifice the happiness of you and your loved ones to the greater good of the society. I, myself sometimes follow the same! My family has trained me to be such. But, with my growing age, I refuse to believe that. I want to believe, if you think about the society more and less about your loved ones, you are a good person for the society, but your family has to be the victim of you being good. Like Sita did, like Urmila did, like even Lav and Kush did. Why? The most disappointing part for me was Ram abandoning Sita without even informing her, with a lie. What a kind of a righteous king he is, if he cannot admit in front of his wife that he is abandoning her fearing the society! And he did with a trickery. While reading the book, I was imagining, if by destiny I get a husband/ partner like Ram. Oh no! One who will pour unconditional love to me, but will not take a stand for me if the society will point a finger on me. Forget about taking a stand, imagine he leaving me just because the society is pointing at me. I pray no women in this earth be given a husband liken Ram. Once I read this whole book , now I understand why feminist people always dislike Ram. I am not such strong feminist to dislike him. I, too will say Ram knowingly or unknowingly become a flag bearer of patriarchy.


---- She Donkey
