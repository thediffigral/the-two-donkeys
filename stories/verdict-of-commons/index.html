Written on 2026-09-07, after going through The Owl the River and the Valley - Arupa Patangia Kalita.

verdict of commons

 The last stories of the Arupa Kalita books are done. While the last second was of normal length, the last most one are quiet a lengthy one and yes, to be honest, i could not think about where the story is leading to. Soon after 20%-30%, I understood the story can very well be used to understand the situation the Majuli people goes through. The story was more like a container of sub-stories, like some of the web-series, where suppose a team solves detective cases. each episode hence is a new case, new environment- but then the detective team also has a running story, so people will go to watch the next episode not in search of new detective story, but for the running story the central character team has. in the story, it is about the lady soon to give birth a child and the trauma she has borne while travelling through a boat in Brahmaputra.  The second last story had another taste, where the wife of the powerful aura husband gaining the aura of the husband (atleast from the perspective of her side characters) and the carried relationship with people and things thereafter. i don't know why, while I was reading, the leading female character was portrayed as the rajmata of bahubali movie (by my mind). 

I liked the book. from 12 featured stoies, i liked 9 of them, probably 3 of them i could not grasp the intended meaning. while I feel good that i could delve into Arupa Kalita's short stories (earlier i only did read her a novel for the adolscents), but i also feel i should have a read all of these in Assamese itself. the translator Mitra Phukan, atleast in all these stories really did a excellent job, however, i would not comment on it any further because 1) i am not an expert in translation works, 2) the assameser version of the stories i have not yet gone through. to judge a book one don't necessarily need to be an expert, a general perspective matters more for an author (because if the common people did not get your message, the world doesnot need only the experts). what i read from this book can be more of Phukan's style rather the authentic assamese touch by Kalita - it's 'can be' or 'may be'. nonetheless, i also feel i should get into more books from assamese authors, particularly the old ones. 

thank you to both Arupa Patangia Kalita and Mitra Phukan for the book. 


-- Male Donkey.
