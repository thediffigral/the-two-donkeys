who owns the war?

 i grabbed the second story because i felt too hungry to know what she is upto. often it happens to me when i read a book, or to be precise a collection of short stories - the first one attracts me the most, and later i found that's the only piece that was good. this is a very sad moment i feel and i don't want to have that given an option. Banu's next story i started to read keeping in mind that i am not expecting any carry-over from the previous story. i went on, and with each page, i got hooked real bad. the story is again set to the environment familiar to the previous one, but it had its own charm. i did a speed read and gradually i felt i forgot what was happening surrounding my environment. the story was really good becuase it indeed gave me the micro-detailing surprise reveal feel at the end - the kind of ending i myself want to write and appreciate when others also write. to be precise i got kidnapped, (i mean my mind) by Banu, and she actually took control of my mind and let it go where she wanted to. i really loved this fact that she is one kind of writer i found. i believe sometimes it's really true that you meet/find/attract/know similar people, by accident (most), willful or maybe by anyhow, annyc hance. (but i could not get the chance to mee BNS, probably he is beyond my personality , (obviously!) (however the writings is where we meet, like in this case)).  I mean after reading the story, i felt like i lost to Banu and i will admit it that i got this that i don't want to lose it too easily. but that yes, 'Fire Rain' has that. 

i wanted to say that because I did a speed read, that s why i missed it and it is in 'not my mother tongue', but then when i remember the ending of the story, i actually felt what the protagonist felt - so in the compeititon of this two scale, Banu is the ultimate winner.
