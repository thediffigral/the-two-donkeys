Written on 2026-09-05, after going through The Owl the River and the Valley - Arupa Patangia Kalita.

does this thought exist?

 I really liked the next three stories from Arupa Kalita's book. Unlike the previous two, where I found myself wondering in thoughts in between, these were well structured to keep my mind hooked. I noticed the translator has done it in a way that the sentences give me a feeling of completion of something each time, for example, 'she has done it this way. also she bought something. then this..' probably because i am constantly seeking the assameseness in the stories. maybe i am not able to write out what i am actually carrying in mind. maybe the feeling is like : we had to translate sentences in our school exams in the english subject. there were random assamese sentences to test our grammer understanding. so there will be sentences like, 'it has been raining since morning', 'he has done the work', something similar. so, probably when i found such formation of sentences in the stories, my past triggered me similar response. this is happening only with the recent stories i read, not the preivous one. 

nonethesless the stories centered around woman in the centre, and their tragic lifeline as a whole. i just thought often in a short story collection, we read writings that features normal day-to-day life encounters, feelings etc and most of the times, the stories we are touched by the deep feeling, the un observant inner thoughts the character feels (and we resonate the same). these stories are hardly any comic one. indeed, the comic stories are mostly featured in a separate book, highlighting the book to be a featured comic stories (or byongo xahityo) .. we don't have such miserable stories (or krndon xahityo) - maybe there, but I am not aware.
