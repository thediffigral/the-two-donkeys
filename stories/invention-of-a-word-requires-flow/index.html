invention of a word requires flow

 amidst the prepation of an exam, i casually decided to read a book rather scrllong through another youtube video: a habit i wanted to grow from a long time. the book I picked is not random, i first heard about the author, that she won booker prize this year, Banu Mushtaq. i searched for the epub version of the book heart lamp:selected stories, for whch she has won the title. i staretd the first story it has, and it is profoundly beautiful. written in first person view, i first did not pay attention about the gender of the protagonist (actually i did not look into the gender of Banu), then eventually i found out. the story progresses in a very casual and gives a warm-homely feeling, and as it progreesess i could not stop. I like the fact that how she introduces new character in a connected way, for example, she talks about her grandmother and how she used to scold, and in the next moment, there is a news of her grandmother died. in the story at first the scolding of grandmother feels like 'is it needed at this point, how is this relevent to the story? ' then in the next progression of the story, the reader realises (to be precise, I realised). it is written in indian english style, so I did not feel that much uncomfortness (is it a valid word? i don't know). 

-- Male Donkey
